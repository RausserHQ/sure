# Sure

Sure helps a household understand its financial position and activity. Account activity, household reporting and changes in debt describe different aspects of the same financial events.

## Language

**Provider observation**:
A value reported by an external financial source, together with its source, time and stated meaning. A reported value can be known even when its accounting meaning remains unresolved.

**Ledger direction**:
The direction of an account transaction under Sure's signed-amount convention. It does not by itself establish household income, expense or a change in debt.

**Reporting classification**:
The economic meaning of activity for the household, such as income, expense, financing, refund, transfer or unresolved activity. A relationship between accounts does not erase independently established economic meaning.

**Transfer relationship**:
The association of activity representing movement between accounts. Related activity can have independently established reporting meanings and balance effects.

**Balance quantity**:
The particular financial quantity being measured, such as principal outstanding, accrued interest, total liability or available credit. Values measuring different quantities are not interchangeable.
_Avoid_: Balance without an identified quantity when discussing liability reconciliation.

**Principal outstanding**:
The unpaid principal of a debt. Accrued interest and fees are separate unless they have been capitalized into principal.
_Avoid_: Total liability as a synonym.

**Balance effect**:
The signed change an activity directly causes in a specified balance quantity. A known zero effect is different from an unknown effect, and its amount can differ from the activity's ledger amount.

**Balance anchor**:
An accepted absolute value for a specific quantity at an established point in time. It supports reconstruction only where the intervening effects and their coverage are established.

**Observed history**:
Values measured at particular times. An interval without enough evidence remains a gap.

**Reconstructed history**:
Values derived from an accepted anchor and a complete set of known effects for the interval. The explanation identifies both the anchor and the effects used.

**Projection**:
Expected future values calculated from assumptions such as payment schedules and interest rates. A projection is not evidence of settled activity.
